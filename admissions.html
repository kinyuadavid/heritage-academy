Admissions

Places for 2027 are open.

Playgroup to Grade 9. Four steps, no agents, no hidden charges. Fill the form below and the admissions office calls you within two working days.

How to join

From first call to first day.

  1. Tell us about your child

    Fill the form on this page, call the office, or send a WhatsApp. We need the child's name, the grade they are joining and your phone number.

  2. Visit the school

    Come on any school day before 4pm, or to an open day. Bring the last report form if you have it. No appointment needed.

  3. A short placement test

    From Grade 1 upwards: reading, numbers and a short chat with the deputy, about an hour. For Playgroup and PP1 there is no test at all, just a morning in the class with us.

  4. Accept the place

    Pay the admission fee and the first instalment, collect the joining letter and the uniform list, and the child starts on the opening day.

    Questions parents ask

    The honest answers.

    Do you take learners mid-term?

    Yes, where there is room in the class. A child who joins mid-term is assessed the same week and gets two weeks of catch-up lessons at no charge.

    Is there transport for day learners?

    Two buses run morning and evening: one through Wajir town and Makoror, one along the Griftu road. Transport is 4,500 a term, and the little ones are handed to a parent at the stop, never left.

    What happens if fees are late?

    No child is sent home on the first day of a delay. The bursar agrees a written plan with you. We would rather keep the child in class and talk.

    How young do you take them?

    Playgroup from three years old, provided the child is out of nappies. PP1 at four, PP2 at five. The early years block has its own gate, yard and nap room.

    When can a child start boarding?

    Grade 4, and not before. The youngest boarders have their own dormitory, their own matron and an earlier lights out than the Grade 9 learners.

    Is the school religious?

    The school is mixed. Both Christian and Islamic religious education are taught, Friday prayers are observed, and nobody is pushed either way.
